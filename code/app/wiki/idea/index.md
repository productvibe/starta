A work app is a custom app you build for your own job. It keeps one set of
data about your work, puts a structured screen on top of it, and Claude keeps
that data current. You own it, run it, build it and use it.

This section explains the idea. It answers "what is this, and why would I
want one?" before the [Learn](/wiki/learn) course shows you how to build it.

## The three parts

| Part | What it is | In your app |
|---|---|---|
| One set of data | Everything the app knows, kept in files on your laptop | One data file per module, such as `tasks.json` and `people.json`; Markdoc pages for About and the wiki |
| A structured screen | Pages you open in your browser to read and change that data | The sidebar, the tables, the task list, the wiki |
| Kept current by Claude | Claude reads and writes the files when you ask | You say "Add Maya Okafor to People", Claude writes the row, the page reloads |

Most tools give you one of these. A spreadsheet gives you data with a weak
screen. A chat with Claude gives you help with no data that lasts. A work app
joins the three, for one person and one job.

## What you start with

Your app starts with up to five modules in the sidebar. You pick them on Create; About is always in:

| Module | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Tasks | What you have to do, as a list you tick off |
| People | Who you work with, as a table |
| Goals | What you are aiming for this year, and whether each is on track |
| Wiki | This explainer, the course, and what you learn about your work |
| About | Your role, written by Claude after it interviews you |

None of them needs a connector, so they all work in your first hour with data
you type or paste.

## A worked example

Sam is a customer success manager at Brightlane, a software company. They are
new in the role. In their first hour they:

1. paste the setup prompt from starta.dev/create into the Code tab, and Claude makes their work app and opens it in their browser;
2. answer Claude's questions about their role, and Claude writes their About page;
3. add the goals they are measured on, such as renewals closed and healthy accounts;
4. add the people they work with at Tallow & Co and Harbourline Logistics;
5. list this week's follow-ups in Tasks;
6. design their own module, Accounts, with the columns they want.

Sam does not write code. They describe what they want in the Code tab of the
Claude desktop app, Claude changes the files, and they check the result in
the browser. By the end of the hour they have something they will open tomorrow.

{% callout title="The words" %}
A **module** is what you see in the sidebar. A **block** is the reusable piece
a module is built from: Tasks and People are two modules on one block, the
datatable. The full list is in [Words](/wiki/reference/words).
{% /callout %}
